The vision

Football should create possibilities, not protect privilege.

The biggest nations will always bring quality and history. But the world's game should also make room for new stories, smaller nations and the unexpected.

01 / THE CALENDAR

A season that respects life

Run club football from February to November, with December and January set aside for rest, family time and preparation. Schedule summer kick-offs with heat and supporter comfort in mind.

02 / THE WORLD CUP

Invite the whole world

Invite every FIFA member association. No qualifying campaign. Every willing participant gets at least three group-stage matches, with financial help for nations that cannot afford to take part.

03 / THE DRAW

One draw. No protection.

No seeded pots, no protected favourites and no artificial balancing. A completely random draw gives every team a path—and lets the football decide what happens next.

04 / REFEREEING

Technology that helps

Use technology to reduce preventable errors, explain decisions clearly and support referees without turning every match into a stop-start review session.

05 / INTERNATIONAL FOOTBALL

Fewer interruptions

Coordinate national-team football into clearer windows where possible, reducing repeated disruption to club seasons while preserving meaningful international competition.

06 / CONTINENTAL RULES

Clear, consistent eligibility

Use transparent geographic criteria for continental competitions, with published definitions, consistent application and a fair process for disputed cases.

The principles

Give every nation a chance.
Give every player a break.
Let the game decide.

This is not a promise that every match will be close or every country will win. It is a call to make opportunity wider, rules more transparent and football's future worthy of its global reach.

OpportunityA genuine chance to participate.
Fair treatmentNo special protection for favourites.
Player welfareRest, recovery and sensible scheduling.
IntegrityClear rules and fewer preventable errors.
Why this matters

“Football should not belong only to the countries that are already powerful.”

For some countries, simply walking onto the World Cup pitch would be a historic achievement. Their players and supporters deserve to dream about that possibility, not be excluded automatically by financial barriers or a system that only recognises established powers.

A tournament this ambitious would require serious planning: venues, travel, safety, recovery time, funding and a format that can actually work. Those challenges should be studied honestly. They are reasons to develop a practical plan—not reasons to stop asking what football could become.

The proposals here are a starting point for public discussion. They should be tested, challenged and improved by supporters, players, clubs and football organisations.
